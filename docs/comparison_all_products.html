Where DiscForge leads

  • Breadth in one tool. No other single product spans preservation-grade reading, RAW-DAO burning, format conversion, cross-family filesystem reading, Redump workflow, audio ripping, CHD, and DVD/BD authoring. The field is specialised — DiscForge is the union.
  • RAW-DAO burning with protection re-creation, validated byte-for-byte against golden builds via sub-channel-Q consensus read-back. Among preservation tools this is almost unique (Aaru and DIC don't burn); the only other RAW-DAO writers are legacy (cdrdao) or commercial copy tools (Alcohol/CloneCD) with no verification grading.
  • "Provably correct or declined" verification. Empty/short read-backs fail rather than pass; consensus never blends a forged sub-channel Q; unsupported structures decline instead of emitting maybe-corrupt output. This review pass hardened that ethos further across the filesystem, CHD, ROM and burn-verify paths.
  • Filesystem reader coverage now includes FAT, exFAT, NTFS, ext2/3/4, HFS, ISO9660/Joliet/Rock Ridge and UDF — matching IsoBuster/Aaru on the mainstream families, with synthetic-image tests for each.
  • Forensics & provenance depth — entropy, fuzzy hashing, disc genome/genealogy, rot/health mapping, provenance merges — goes beyond what any single competitor exposes.

Where competitors still lead (honest gaps)

  • Aaru — raw filesystem/format breadth & compressed AaruFormat. Aaru reads ~60 filesystems (including tape and flux-level formats DiscForge doesn't touch) and decodes compressed AaruFormat (LZMA/FLAC). DiscForge now reads and writes uncompressed AaruFormat correctly and writes CICM metadata, but still declines compressed AaruFormat — faithful LZMA/FLAC decode needs a real Aaru image as an oracle to validate against, which is the honest blocker on that last step.
  • DiscImageCreator — submission trust & drive matrix. DIC is the accepted Redump submission standard with the widest tested-drive support and years of community-validated protection dumping. DiscForge's RAW path is proven on Plextor hardware but doesn't yet carry DIC's breadth of drive support or submission acceptance.
  • IsoBuster — recovery UX. DiscForge has the recovery engines (fs-recover, salvage-plan, recover-toc, bad-sectors, scratch-verdict, health/rot maps); IsoBuster's edge is two decades of GUI polish for pulling data off damaged/odd media.
  • EAC / dBpoweramp — secure-ripping depth. DiscForge exposes the building blocks (AccurateRip, read-offset/offset-detect, C2, read-stability); EAC's edge is deeper combined drive-offset/error-recovery heuristics and tight encoder integration.
  • Openness. Aaru, DIC, cdrtools and CHDMAN are open source; DiscForge is proprietary — a licensing/business decision, not a technical gap.

Update — August 2026 session

Three cells moved this pass after verifying against the codebase and running it on Linux: CHD is now full — DiscForge creates CHDs (accepted by chdman), not just reads them. Cross-platform is upgraded to partial — the file/analysis CLI builds and runs natively on Linux (verified this session by reading a real mke2fs-created ext4 image byte-for-byte); only the hardware burn/read layer stays Windows-only (SPTI), pending a Linux SG_IO backend. Aaru interop gained an uncompressed AaruFormat writer (round-trips through DiscForge's own reader), so DiscForge now both reads and produces the interchange format — the remaining gap is compressed-block decode.

Bottom line

Against any single competitor, DiscForge matches or exceeds on nearly every axis that overlaps, and it uniquely combines verified preservation reading with verified RAW-DAO burning. The remaining honest gaps are specialisation gaps — Aaru's sheer filesystem/format breadth and compressed-AaruFormat decode, DIC's community-standard submission trust and drive matrix, chdman's maximally-compressed CHD codecs, and EAC's audio-ripping heuristic depth — plus the strategic choice of being proprietary and (for hardware I/O) Windows-only. Nothing in the field does what DiscForge does end-to-end; the closest, Aaru, still doesn't burn.
